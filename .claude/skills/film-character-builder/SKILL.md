# Film Character Builder

Create people whose particular wants and choices generate the story. Develop observable specificity rather than an accumulation of biography or attractive traits.

## Establish the character's job in the story

Read the premise, runtime, genre, existing script, user corrections and selected character references. Separate established facts from proposals and open casting choices. Preserve the approved plot and identity when revising an existing character. Flag a necessary upstream change instead of silently rewriting the story.

Use [the character bible](assets/character-bible.md) for a substantial character or ensemble; scale it down for a small role. Start with immediate want, stakes, obstacle, tactic and a choice that affects what happens next. Give supporting and opposing characters aims that exist beyond serving the protagonist.

## Make the individual specific

Choose a credible tension between public behaviour and private pressure, or between two values. Make it visible in an action or relationship. Add relevant competence, an ordinary personal detail and a behaviour that changes under pressure. Do not make trauma, a secret, a scar or a redemption arc mandatory. Compelling characters can be joyful, capable, principled, mistaken, funny or steadfast.

Build relationships reciprocally: what each wants, knows, misreads and accurately recognises about the other. Differentiate characters through choices, attention, sentence shape and tactics, not only appearance or demographic labels. Do not infer accent, temperament, culture or history from a reference person's face.

For a short film, retain only the past needed to understand the present choice. Do not force the script to explain an extensive biography. Test whether the ending exposes or changes something established at the opening. A character may change the situation while holding to a value rather than undergo a personal conversion.

## Connect identity to production

Record a stable character ID, selected portrait and version. Treat physical design choices as proposals when casting is open. Avoid silently replacing an approved face when changing age presentation, styling or mood. For different life stages, track what changes and what remains recognisable; do not reduce ageing or changing circumstances to stereotypes.

Provide a styling handoff about self-presentation, practical work, possessions and relevant life stages. Provide a voice handoff distinguishing confirmed choices from open casting. Provide an acting handoff with tactics and triggers rather than generic emotion labels. Use dedicated styling, voice, performance and script skills when available for those downstream tasks; the character bible should not invent technical controls or authorise renders.

## Deliver and check

Return a usable character record, relationship pressure, observable arc and concise downstream handoffs. Mark alternatives and unapproved history as proposals. Check that the character makes a consequential choice, their contradiction appears on screen, their behaviour fits the world and their essential story can be understood within the runtime. If a description could fit everyone, replace its generic traits with individual decisions.
