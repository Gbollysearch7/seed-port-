# Film Character Performance

Turn an emotional intention into behaviour caused by events in the scene. Support quiet, energetic, comic, intimate and confrontational performances; do not make restraint or shouting the default.

## Establish the performance problem

Read the current scene, user corrections, selected take and continuity state. Preserve approved dialogue, story, character identity, physical abilities, wardrobe, voice identity and blocking. Distinguish a requested change of delivery from a request to rewrite the scene. For a missing creative choice, make a clearly labelled proposal; do not silently remove a requested movement or emotional peak.

For a critique, distinguish the user's observations from what you personally reviewed. A still cannot establish response timing, vocal intensity or lip-sync.

## Direct the character's action

For each important turn, establish:

- What the character wants from whom, what it costs to fail, and what they are concealing.
- The specific line, event or realisation that changes their behaviour.
- Their tactic before and after that trigger: persuade, dismiss, reassure, challenge, entice, plead, expose, protect, withdraw or another playable action.
- The visible or audible response and the consequence that remains after it.

Treat emotion, vocal force and physical energy as independent choices. High feeling need not mean shouting; when the user requests shouting, embodied delivery and breath pressure must change rather than only playback volume. Do not suppress approved intensity through habitual “subtle,” “restrained,” “hands still” or “no movement” instructions.

Choose a few causally connected cues from gaze, expression, breath, speech, hands, posture and locomotion. Avoid listing every bodily channel for every second. Give gestures a target, purpose and completion; give movement a start mark, route and endpoint. Allow the character to walk, turn, rise, reach or react broadly when appropriate. Do not add continuous fidgeting to satisfy a request for life.

Direct listeners as well as speakers. They register, resist, reinterpret and decide; they do not merely wait motionless for their line. Tie reactions to their causes and avoid rigid blink or breath schedules. Maintain bodily and emotional recovery after a peak rather than resetting to neutral.

Use [the behaviour palette](references/behaviour-palette.md) when generating alternatives, mixed emotions or a targeted repair. Its examples are optional creative choices, not universal emotional signs or mandatory character traits.

## Make the direction filmable

Use [the performance sheet](assets/character-performance.md) for a reusable brief or a complex exchange; omit irrelevant fields for simple shots. Align each essential gesture with a framing that can show it. Camera movement is not a substitute for acting. If the blocking must change, identify the change and its continuity consequences.

For multi-shot prompts, specify practical time windows, exact speaker-bound dialogue, listening beats and edit cues. Leave room for action and recovery. Separate the character's stable voice identity from this scene's delivery. Keep provider parameters outside the creative performance brief; this skill does not mandate a model or resolution.

For spoken scenes, use the [voice, accent and pacing sheet](assets/voice-accent-pacing.md) to preserve the selected identity and accent while varying delivery. Budget speech, pauses, response and holds on one timeline, counting concurrent action once. A camera cut need not interrupt a sentence. Do not infer accent from appearance or silently change it during an emotional peak. Where available, film-voice-designer handles detailed voice design and film-dialogue-writer handles authorised wording changes.

At a generation handoff, record actual eyeline, posture, weight, both hands, props, wardrobe state, breath, last words and residual feeling. Continue from that state without repeating dialogue or resetting the performance. Use the project's requested hold duration when applicable: settle the deliberate gesture and camera while breathing and attention continue. Holds are edit handles, not mandatory duplicated pauses in a final cut. A two-second hold or 480p restriction from one film must not become a universal rule.

Compile only the selected direction into the model prompt: starting state → trigger → response and tactic → exact line and delivery → partner's reaction → consequence → next-shot state. Do not submit the entire option palette, contradictory acting choices or vague prestige labels such as “Oscar-level” as a substitute for direction.

## Review and revise

Check causality, readable tactics, range, listening, speech intelligibility, gesture completion, purposeful movement and continuity. Separate intentional stillness from a character who seems unresponsive. Record whether review used playback, audio, sampled images or automated checks; do not claim unobserved acting quality.

For a live-action realism target, verify that expression, breath, speech effort and physical action belong to the same ongoing event. Inspect contact, balance, offscreen listening and the recovery after emotional peaks. Do not add regular blinks, constant fidgeting, forced asymmetry or random imperfections as a substitute for motivated acting. Preserve individuality and expressive range. If playback is unavailable, leave timing and performance believability unverified rather than claiming the scene looks genuinely filmed. Where available, film-video-prompter supplies the broader physical, photographic, sound and sequence review.

For a failed take, name the observed defect and change the smallest relevant cue, timing window or coverage choice. Preserve successful elements and version prompts and references. Writing or updating a performance brief does not authorise paid generation or automatic retries. Return the usable performance direction, compact prompt and material continuity notes; label unrendered examples as drafts.
