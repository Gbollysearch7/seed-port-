## Character's through-line

Complete for each character, including the listener.

- **Want:** What must the other person do, admit, believe or feel?
- **Stakes:** What does failing to get it cost them here?
- **Private feeling / public behaviour:** What do they feel, and what are they trying to show or conceal?
- **Starting state → turning point → ending state:**
- **Tactic:** A playable verb addressed to someone: reassure, challenge, persuade, dismiss, entice, test, plead, conceal, accuse, comfort, celebrate. Change it when the first tactic fails.
- **Personal behaviour:** One relevant habit or physical tendency, used when motivated. Avoid assigning the same jaw clench, head tilt or nod to every character.

For each important beat, set these **independently** if useful:

- Emotional intensity: low / medium / high; what is at stake beneath the surface?
- Vocal force: soft / conversational / projected / raised / shouted; what makes it change?
- Physical energy: contained / active / expansive; what movement carries it?

High emotion can be silent, and a loud line can conceal fear. Do not use those possibilities to flatten a requested outburst or energetic scene.

## Gesture and movement choices

Select a small number of useful cues, not every cue in the list. Choose from the [behaviour palette](../references/behaviour-palette.md) when needed.

- **Eyes / face:** Who or what receives the look? What changes after the trigger? Let mouth and expression release between thoughts.
- **Breath / speech:** Intake, interrupted exhale, pressure, pace, emphasis, pauses and recovery. Keep character voice identity stable as delivery changes.
- **Hands / prop:** Anatomical left or right hand; starting position → action and purpose → contact or release → resting position. State any prop transfer explicitly.
- **Body / feet:** Start mark and weight → motivated step, turn, lean, recoil, sit, rise, walk or run → route → final mark and orientation. Distinguish anatomical direction from screen direction.
- **Listening:** What do they register, resist or reinterpret before answering? An aborted reply, delayed glance or changed weight may carry the beat. A silent listener does not mouth the speaker's words.
- **Recovery:** What survives the peak—breathing, unfinished impulse, gaze, distance, or a changed tactic? Avoid an instant return to neutral.

Stillness is available when it communicates an active choice. Camera movement does not replace character behaviour. Standing in place does not require locking every joint; physical liveliness does not require constant pacing.

## Closing hold and next-shot handoff

- Hold needed? Why, and for how long? For the current TELL THEM workflow, use the requested **two-second living hold** at generation handoffs; this is a project choice, not a rule for every film.
- By the hold's start, finish the specified gesture and settle camera travel. Retain breathing, balance and attentive gaze; do not command a frozen face or body.
- Record: exact last words; who is about to respond; residual emotion and breath; eyeline; head and torso orientation; foot position and weight; both hands; props; wardrobe; screen position; camera state.
- Begin the next generated shot from that state without repeating the last line or resetting the emotion. A different next angle must preserve the same moment.
- Generation holds are edit handles. In the final film, trim duplicate settling time or cut to the next useful angle if the repeated hold exposes a seam.

## Compact prompt to compile

> [CHARACTER] begins [specific physical and emotional state], trying to [tactic] [ADDRESSEE]. When [visible or audible trigger], [brief reaction] leads to [chosen action]. During [time window], [CHARACTER] says exactly, “[DIALOGUE],” with [vocal change and emphasis]. [One or two visible cues that carry the action, including relevant hand/prop or movement endpoint]. [LISTENER] registers [specific meaning] and responds [observable listening behaviour] after the trigger. After the line, [consequence and recovery]. The camera [approved coverage that shows the important behaviour]. End [state and living hold if needed], carrying [unresolved feeling] into the next shot.

Keep only the highest-value cues in the submitted prompt. Add necessary beats for an approved complex action; do not reduce ambition to make every shot contain only one movement.

## Review and revision

- Does the response happen after its cause, with a readable change of tactic?
- Do voice, breath, gaze and body belong to the same moment? Do both characters listen?
- Is the gesture motivated and visible in the chosen framing? Does it end rather than loop?
- Are intensity and movement varied for a reason? Is a requested raised exchange actually performed, rather than merely louder in the mix?
- Are the line, movement, reaction and hold plausible within the available duration?
- Does the next shot inherit emotion, posture, props and momentum?
- Record what was actually reviewed: continuous playback, audio, sampled images or automated checks. Stills cannot establish timing or lip-sync.
- If a take fails, name the observed defect, retain what worked, and revise the smallest relevant cue. Record prompt and reference versions. This sheet does not authorise paid renders or automatic retries.
