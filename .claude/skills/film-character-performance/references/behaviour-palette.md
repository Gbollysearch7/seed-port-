# Emotion, gesture and movement palette

These are creative options, not universal signs of an emotion. Interpret them through the character, relationship, culture, physical abilities and scene. Choose a tactic and trigger first; then select a few cues that express that choice. Do not paste the whole palette into a video prompt.

## Range of playable choices

| Feeling / situation | What the character tries to do | Contained option | Expressive option and movement | Change or aftermath |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anger at being dismissed | Force the partner to acknowledge the point | Hold a reply, then make one word unusually precise | Project the answer, open a palm toward the partner, shift weight forward or take an established step | The hand settles; pressure remains in the next breath |
| Hurt / betrayal | Make the partner recognise the injury | A reply stops; the gaze searches the partner before the next sentence | Turn away to compose, then turn back and name the harm; the voice briefly breaks if appropriate | The accusation gives way to a demand or boundary |
| Fear / apprehension | Find safety, information or time | Stop an ordinary task and check the relevant exit or person | Retreat, move behind cover, call out or pull someone along when the scene warrants it | Listening remains alert after movement stops |
| Panic | Get help or regain control | Attempt a simple action twice with different results | Rapid movement toward a specific goal, urgent calls, disrupted breathing and an interrupted sentence | A new plan changes the rhythm; avoid endless undirected flailing |
| Grief | Get through an ordinary task or resist a fact | Handle a familiar object, pause when its significance arrives | Reach for the absent person's place, fold into support or cry with a physically continuous onset | Give the person time to breathe and attempt the next action |
| Shame / embarrassment | Hide exposure or repair how they are seen | Begin an explanation, abandon it, choose where to look | A laugh escapes at the wrong moment; step aside, correct the mistake, or over-explain | The cover fails or becomes an honest admission |
| Guilt / remorse | Acknowledge responsibility or seek repair | Let the defence die unfinished and meet the partner's eyes | Return an object, undo an action or make a clear admission with open posture | The partner's response changes what repair is possible |
| Joy / delight | Share good news or draw someone into it | Check the news again, then let recognition spread into a smile | Laugh, rise quickly, reach out, bounce or cross the space for a greeting if approved | Let breath and movement settle into connection |
| Relief | Confirm that the danger or uncertainty has passed | Release held breath only after confirmation | Shoulders drop, laugh through an exhale, sit or lean on an established support | Residual trembling or disbelief may remain; calm need not be immediate |
| Affection / tenderness | Reassure, attend to or reconnect | Make room, listen closely, offer rather than impose a touch | Move nearer, embrace, help with a task or respond warmly when consistent with the relationship and blocking | Track contact, release and changed distance |
| Desire / attraction | Invite a response while managing exposure | Hold a look, test a small invitation, watch whether it is returned | Close some of the established distance or make an explicit invitation | Let the other person's response determine the next action |
| Suspicion / jealousy | Test an explanation or uncover what is withheld | Pause an activity and ask a deceptively plain question | Reposition to see the relevant object or person; challenge a contradiction directly | Curiosity becomes accusation, reassurance or withdrawal based on evidence |
| Confidence / pride | Lead, persuade or enjoy recognition | Finish an action without rushing, then address the listener | Claim space, demonstrate, welcome others into a celebration | Confidence changes when challenged; avoid a permanent smirk |
| Surprise / disbelief | Verify what just happened | Interrupt a prepared answer and look again | Recoil, laugh, step toward the evidence or ask a sharp question | Choose the next tactic after recognition; avoid a held open-mouth pose |
| Frustration / impatience | Overcome a specific obstacle | Reset the task with a tighter, more deliberate attempt | Put the tool down, stand, change position or demand a direct answer | A solution or refusal changes the energy |
| Playfulness / comic embarrassment | Tease, impress or recover dignity | Delay the answer and see whether the partner notices | Overcommit to a gesture, catch the mistake, then attempt a plausible recovery | The response supplies timing; do not add a smile or wink to every beat |

Tears, trembling, smiling and gaze avoidance are choices, not compulsory emotional codes. A character can hide an emotion, misread another person, or behave against expectation. Specify why.

## Mixed emotions and changes of tactic

- **Anger covering shame:** dismiss the criticism confidently; a concrete fact interrupts the defence; answer too forcefully; fail to sustain the certainty afterward.
- **Joy mixed with grief:** begin sharing the good news; look toward the person who is missing; let the celebration change without erasing it.
- **Affection with a firm boundary:** respond warmly to recognition; keep the established distance; deliver the refusal clearly; remain emotionally present.
- **Fear turning into resolve:** check the obstacle; stop retreating; choose a route and take the first deliberate action while still afraid.
- **Relief turning into anger:** release the held breath when the person returns safely; only then confront what their absence cost.

## Movement vocabulary with a purpose

- **Approach / withdrawal:** mark → route → destination; state what the move seeks or refuses. Track screen direction and the partner's response.
- **Turn / interruption:** begin toward a task or exit; a specific line arrests the turn; decide whether to face the speaker or continue.
- **Sit / rise / lean:** establish the seat or support, approach, weight transfer and final balance. Show the movement in a suitable shot.
- **Pacing:** give the pace a boundary and a reason; let a discovery interrupt it. Do not prescribe repeated back-and-forth motion merely to add energy.
- **Reach / offer / recoil:** identify hand, target, distance, contact, release and object ownership. An unanswered offer can be the emotional action.
- **Stillness under pressure:** the person actively withholds a reply or movement; name what makes that restraint costly and what eventually changes it.

## Worked example — raised exchange, not a fixed default

**Draft performance example, 15 seconds; no render requested.** Two former partners remain on established marks, Leah screen left and Daniel screen right. Daniel has a phone in his anatomical right hand at his thigh; Leah's bag remains on its established shoulder. The opening state carries the preceding accusation. Existing dialogue is unchanged.

**0–2 s — receive the accusation.** In Daniel's medium shot, he starts to dismiss her, then realises she is still waiting for an answer. His breath catches before he answers. His task becomes to reject the debt she says he owes; he looks at her, not toward camera.

**2–5 s — his defence breaks into raised voice.** Daniel: “I never asked you to do any of that!” Pressure grows on “asked”; the line is projected toward her with intelligible consonants. His left palm opens once low at waist level; weight moves forward without leaving the mark. The phone remains in his right hand. Do not substitute gain added to an otherwise quiet delivery.

**5–7 s — let the effect reach her.** Cut to Leah as she receives the dismissal. She takes a quick breath, begins an answer, then commits to making him acknowledge the ordinary fact of eating what she provided. Anger grows out of hearing him, not before his line has landed.

**7–11 s — she meets his force with her own tactic.** Leah: “You didn't have to ask. You ate, didn't you?” Her pace is direct, with the strongest emphasis on “ate.” One low open-handed gesture indicates the plainness of the fact rather than pointing in his face. Her expression asks whether he can deny it. Daniel listens offscreen; no additional words.

**11–13 s — consequence.** Cut to Daniel. His attempted answer stops before becoming a word. His left hand returns toward his side. He remains angry, but cannot use the same defence; his breath and focus carry that conflict.

**13–15 s — living hold.** Keep this reaction in a settled frame. His lips are closed, breath remains visible, eyes stay on Leah, and the phone stays low in his right hand. Continue the next shot from this state. This is a proposed performance to evaluate in playback, not proof of the model's acting or timing.

## Repair the observed failure

| Observed result | More useful revision |
|---|---|
| Face barely changes despite an important line | Identify the exact meaning that lands, then a visible decision; frame closely enough to read it |
| Body is rigid throughout | Give a motivated physical task, weight change or route that fits the beat and the framing |
| Constant nodding, gesturing or pacing | Keep the gesture that changes the interaction; give it an endpoint and remove unrelated repetitions |
| Loud but emotionally flat | Change tactic, emphasis, breath pressure and response to the partner, not only volume |
| Everyone appears angry in the same way | Separate what each wants and what each conceals; choose different physical and vocal strategies |
| Reaction appears before its trigger | Tie its onset to the audible line or visible event and allow listening time |
| New clip restarts from neutral | Carry the actual previous breath, eyeline, posture and unresolved emotional pressure forward |
| Too many movements fail or merge | Prioritise what tells the story; give necessary actions more time or coverage rather than deleting the story beat |
| A continuity hold looks frozen | End the deliberate gesture while maintaining breathing, balance and attention |
| Joined clips visibly reset | Check actual framing and performance endpoints; trim duplicate lead-ins or use the next motivated angle, with an appropriate audio bridge |
