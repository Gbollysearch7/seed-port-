# Film Cinematography

Translate dramatic intent into a coherent set of photographic choices. The deliverable is an image strategy and shot specifications, not a collection of cinematic adjectives.

## Read the scene

Identify subject priority, point of view, the emotional turn, actor routes, edit neighbors, available location, period, material circumstances and output format. Keep ordinary homes ordinary when that is the story. Strong photography does not require expensive interiors or conspicuous lighting effects.

## Build the visual progression

Choose where the audience begins in relation to the characters and where it ends: outside their exchange, close to one person's attention, or sharing a moment between them. Make changes in scale, angle, focus or camera proximity correspond to that progression. Repeated framing can establish a pattern; break it when the event warrants it.

For each key shot define:

- **Vantage and frame:** camera side, height, subject distance, shot size, headroom/look room, foreground and background relationships. Use foreground objects purposefully; do not obscure faces or the decisive prop action for decorative depth.
- **Optics:** field of view and intended rendering, then a coherent focal-length family if useful. State the assumed format or treat millimeters as approximate visual shorthand. Camera position determines perspective; lens choice affects framing and may motivate a different position. Do not claim that changing focal length alone changes perspective from a fixed position.
- **Focus:** what is sharp, what may soften, and what action cues a focus change. Keep both hands and the relevant object readable during a delicate exchange. Avoid demanding deep focus and an extremely shallow plane on the same subjects at the same time.
- **Light:** source, direction, size/softness, falloff, practicals, shadow detail and background separation. Track the source as camera and performer move. A shadow-side vantage can provide modeling, but must not erase the face's useful expression.
- **Finish:** palette, contrast, highlight treatment and texture across adjoining shots. Grain, halation and lens artifacts are optional choices with a purpose, not a compulsory package.

## Treat motion as one photographic choice

Design the relationship between moving subjects and the frame. Static compositions can contain entrances, changes in distance, occlusion or a transfer of attention. Moving shots can follow a task or reveal information. Do not force an entrance into every scene or a push-in into every emotional beat. For a complex path, use the available film-camera-movement skill after setting photographic priorities.

## Location scouting and coverage sheets

When a location is needed, use [the location bible](assets/location-bible.md) to research visual references, establish one coherent set and derive the required camera angles. FILMS X prefers Savee when useful; use available tools or a documented CLI and inspect actual images. Track source provenance, unseen design decisions, floor-plan geometry and rejected angle variants. A sheet must extend one location rather than redesign it between panels.

## Reference strategy for AI work

Keep identity references readable; place the intended light and finish in location or scene-look references. Use a composed start frame when precise framing, exposure or geometry is important; use separate references when staging freedom is useful. Neither route is mandatory for every shot.

When a location plate has already been generated, inspect whether its angle and lighting can support the proposed camera position and actor route. A new view should extend the same room, not quietly redesign it. Track what is visible, inferred or not yet established.

Actual UI settings, a selected API parameter, an image reference and prose are different forms of control. Check what the chosen provider exposes. A camera brand, aperture or stock name in a prompt expresses an approximation; it does not prove the output was physically rendered through that equipment. Describe the visible result that matters, and evaluate it.

## Deliver and assess

Use [the look and coverage worksheet](references/look-and-coverage.md) when developing a sequence. Provide a concise look statement, shot-specific photographic decisions, continuity implications and the evidence needed to judge the result. Keep the director's staging and approved script intact; identify conflicts before changing them.
