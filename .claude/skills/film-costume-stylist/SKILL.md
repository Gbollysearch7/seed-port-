# Film Costume Stylist

Dress the individual for their story, world and intended self-presentation. Support understated or extravagant, contemporary or period, realistic or invented looks without forcing one house aesthetic on every project.

## Establish what can change

Read character, script, scene activity, time/place, climate, references and user styling preferences. Identify the stage: initial character design, outfit exploration, selected-look development, scene breakdown or revision after footage exists. Preserve selected faces, approved outfits and unaffected continuity. Treat a wardrobe request as design, not permission for shopping or generation.

Clarify only consequential missing context. Otherwise make labelled proposals. When an exact outfit is supplied, develop that outfit before offering alternatives. If styling changes an existing take, identify the affected garments, references, scenes and adjacent shots.

## Style the whole person

Use [the wardrobe bible](assets/wardrobe-bible.md) for a complete look and continuity breakdown. Use [the style planning palette](references/character-styling.md) to explore different aesthetics or life stages. Select silhouette, proportion, fit, construction, colour, fabric, layering, closures, footwear, accessories, grooming and condition as a coherent design.

Explain why the character would choose and use the clothes, including any tension between the desired impression and lived reality. Resources, occupation, age or gender alone do not determine taste. Avoid equating wealth with logos, age with dullness, hardship with dirt, or regional identity with a generic costume. Preserve requested bold, patterned, elaborate and theatrical styles when they serve the brief.

Offer a small number of meaningfully different complete looks when exploration is requested. Distinguish their dramatic readings and practical implications rather than changing only colour. Do not pretend universal expertise: research precise historical dates, regional dress, ceremonies, uniforms and specialist construction from appropriate sources, separating evidence from invention. For an invented world, establish and maintain its clothing logic. Verify current product facts when sourcing helps the brief; do not invent products, prices or availability.

## Research designers when the scene calls for them

Use online research for real designer garments and accessories when the character, occasion or user brief benefits from a branded look. Follow [designer sourcing](references/designer-sourcing.md): establish the dramatic reason, inspect actual product imagery, verify names and construction on official designer pages, and record sources and exact accessory placement. A designer name alone is not an outfit prompt. In FILMS X this research is part of the styling workflow when appropriate; it does not require a separate sourcing request. Preserve selected outfits unless a revision is requested. Design research does not imply buying anything.

## Make it wearable and filmable

Specify key layers and closures, sleeve and hem lengths, tucks, footwear state, accessory sides and prop ownership. Coordinate with hair and makeup without changing identity. Check sitting, walking, reaching and contact required by the scene; support the user's stated mobility, fit and coverage needs. Clothes must hang, fold, fasten and interact with bodies plausibly.

Consider the chosen palette, location, lighting and shot scale. Inspect complex patterns and small details for generated instability; do not impose a universal pattern ban or replace the selected design without explanation. Distinguish a design visible only in a reference from a detail the finished shot can actually show.

Track look IDs and state versions by story day, scene and life stage. Record how wetness, dirt, damage, removal, borrowing and fastenings change, then carry them forward. Emotional change alone need not trigger a new outfit. A garment applied to another character, such as a cape, needs contact and ownership states just like a prop.

## Reference handoff and delivery

Bind separate identity and outfit references to the same stable character ID. In the current FILMS X workflow, wardrobe references are neck-down and portraits are separate; treat this as a project preference. Request useful front, back, side or detail views when they resolve a real design/continuity need. Keep the canonical portrait unchanged and version generative derivatives. Writing a reference prompt does not itself authorise image generation.

Return a complete look with rationale, a garment/state breakdown, reusable wardrobe prompt and material research or continuity gaps. Label proposed designs, generated references and selected looks accurately. Work with character, performance and cinematography handoffs where available; do not redesign the plot, face or camera plan solely to suit the clothes.
