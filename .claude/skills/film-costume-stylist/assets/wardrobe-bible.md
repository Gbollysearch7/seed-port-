# Wardrobe and styling bible

Use the character bible to establish the person before dressing them. This sheet covers costume, personal styling, footwear, accessories and coordinated grooming. Use it during initial design or a later wardrobe revision; preserve already selected identity and story facts.

## Brief and scope

- Project / character ID / look ID / version / status:
- Scene, story day, location, weather, season and activity:
- Age or life stage, confirmed context and intended self-presentation:
- Requested aesthetic, era, occasion and reference images:
- Existing portrait, outfit and prop references to preserve:
- Fit, comfort, mobility, coverage and access needs provided by the user:
- Output wanted: concepts / complete look / continuity breakdown / image prompt / sourcing list:
- Design freedom and locked items:
- Shopping budget and availability constraints, only if sourcing is requested:

Treat missing consequential context as an open choice. A design proposal does not authorise purchases or image/video generation. Verify current availability before making purchasing claims; research precise period, uniform or culturally specific details when required.

## Visual argument

- What does the character want their appearance to communicate?
- What practical or personal detail complicates that impression?
- Why would this individual own and wear these items here?
- Silhouette and proportions:
- Palette and material contrast:
- Relationship to the setting and other characters' looks:
- What changes across the story, and why? An emotional turn does not automatically require a new outfit.

Use [the style planning palette](../references/character-styling.md) for options. Any appropriate style can be the starting point; avoid defaulting every character to muted basics, expensive labels or a fashion shoot.

Remove irrelevant rows. Include functional details: tucked or untucked, sleeve roll, cuff height, fastened or open, pocket contents, belt route, bag strap's anatomical shoulder, jewellery side and footwear state. Distinguish a worn accessory from a handheld prop or a costume applied to another actor.

- Hair / headwear / grooming / makeup coordination:
- Selected identity features that styling must preserve:
- Important details visible at the intended shot size and output resolution:
- Movement test: reaching, sitting, walking, turning, lifting or other required actions:
- Interactions: where another hand touches the garment; how cloth, straps or closures respond:

Fabric should hang and move consistently with its chosen construction. Inspect small repeating patterns for instability in generated footage; simplify or change them only when useful and consistent with the selected look. Patterned, bright and elaborate clothes remain valid creative choices.

Track conditions in story order even when scenes are generated out of order. A coat removed, cape applied, shoe dirtied or shirt wetted remains in that state until a plausible change. Age, wealth, occupation or circumstance can change style without requiring a clichéd makeover. Use previous states and compatible new references when revising an existing take; mark affected shots.

## Reference and generation handoff

| Reference ID / path / version | Role | What it controls | What it must not replace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait | Character identity | Selected face and identity | Outfit decisions |
| Wardrobe view | Look design | Exact clothes, fit, layers and accessories | Canonical face |
| Detail view, if needed | Closure / accessory / fabric | Important construction and state | Overall identity or setting |

For the current FILMS X workflow, keep wardrobe sheets neck-down and portraits separate, explicitly bound to the same character. Include front/back/side or detail views only when useful to the look and continuity. This is a project reference preference, not a requirement for every styling workflow.

```text
CHARACTER: [stable ID; selected portrait reference]
LOOK: [look ID/version; story day, activity and intended presentation]
SILHOUETTE / FIT: [chosen proportions]
GARMENTS: [exact items, colours, fabrics, layering and closures]
FOOTWEAR / ACCESSORIES: [items and anatomical sides]
GROOMING: [selected state; identity invariants]
CONDITION: [specific wear, wetness or damage with narrative cause]
MOVEMENT / CONTACT: [actions the costume must support]
REFERENCE OUTPUT: [neck-down wardrobe view or other requested format]
CONTINUITY: [state carried from or into adjacent shots]
```

## Designer research and accessories

When a branded look suits the scene, research and inspect real items online. Record designer, exact product name/code, garment and accessory details, source URL, checked date and selection status. Bind the full look to the character; track watch wrist, bag shoulder, eyewear state and jewellery count. Describe generated approximations honestly. Check price/stock only when relevant; research does not authorise purchases.

## Review

- Does the whole look fit the individual, story, environment and activity?
- Is each layer wearable, with believable fit, construction and contact?
- Does it support the actor's required movement and interaction?
- Are period, cultural or professional claims supported when precision matters?
- Are labels and status cues doing real story work rather than replacing character design?
- Are face, garment details, accessories and wear states consistent across views and clips?
- Is the design status clear: proposed text, generated reference or selected look?
