# Styling across worlds, occasions and life stages

Use this as a design palette, not a set of costumes assigned to stereotypes. Build a complete wearable look from silhouette, fit, proportion, fabric, palette, layering, footwear, accessories, grooming and condition. A style label is the beginning of the brief.

## Style families and useful decisions

| Starting direction | Choices to establish | Story and practical questions |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday / casual | Garment fit, favourite layers, footwear, care and repetition | What does the person actually do today? What do they keep wearing? |
| Streetwear / sportswear | Volume, technical or soft fabrics, footwear proportions, graphics and layering | Is this personal taste, a community reference or performance clothing? Avoid treating all streetwear as one look |
| Tailoring / business | Jacket structure, trouser line, shirt or alternative layer, closures and shoe formality | What are the actual expectations of this workplace or encounter? |
| Formal / evening / ceremonial | Dress code, silhouette, fabric movement, accessories and grooming | Is the person comfortable in the occasion, performing a role, or resisting it? |
| Luxury / understated or expressive | Construction, material quality, proportion and deliberately chosen detail | What does the person value? Price and conspicuous logos are not the only ways to express taste |
| Workwear / utilitarian | Task-specific layers, pockets, fastenings, protection and wear | Can the character perform the work? Verify specialist uniform or equipment details when accuracy matters |
| Minimal / restrained | Precise fit, a limited palette, texture and negative space | What is the look's intention? Simple clothes can still be expressive |
| Maximal / eclectic | Pattern relationships, colour hierarchy, accessories and controlled focal points | Which combinations belong to this person rather than a random assortment? |
| Romantic / bohemian | Drape, volume, texture, layering and footwear | Does the look suit the climate, movement and setting? |
| Subculture / alternative | Specific scene, place, era, references and personal variation | Research specificity instead of combining unrelated symbols into a generic costume |
| Period / historical | Exact place, date, occasion, silhouette, construction, underlayers and grooming | Research material evidence; separate verified detail from deliberate stylisation |
| Cultural / regional / ceremonial dress | Named context, occasion, wearing method, construction and individual preference | Do not treat an entire continent, religion or nationality as one outfit; verify meaningful details |
| Fantasy / mythic | World logic, craft, materials, social rules, climate and functional closures | How is it made, worn and maintained in this world? Track coherent invented rules |
| Science fiction / futuristic | World technology, material behaviour, work and social context | What genuinely changes from ordinary clothing? Avoid adding arbitrary straps and glowing panels to every look |
| Stage / music / editorial | Audience distance, movement, lighting, concept and intentional exaggeration | Is spectacle the brief? Preserve that ambition while keeping the costume physically workable |

These families can be combined intentionally. They are not exhaustive and do not claim expertise in every historical or cultural tradition. For specific factual claims, use relevant museum collections, archival photographs, designers' documentation or appropriate specialist sources; record the evidence. For current product sourcing, verify availability, sizing, price and location.

## Style through time and circumstance

- **Life stage:** choose what the person retains, replaces or alters as their life changes. Do not dress all older characters alike or change identity simply because age changes.
- **Career and resources:** show individual priorities and practical access, not an automatic poor-to-rich costume switch. A cared-for inexpensive garment and a neglected expensive one can both carry meaning.
- **Public and private:** distinguish what is chosen for work, home, an interview, a celebration, mourning or intimacy through the character's context.
- **Season and environment:** materials, footwear, layers and grooming respond to conditions. Track outerwear changes as actions.
- **Story event:** a removed jacket, loosened collar, borrowed coat or applied work cape can alter the relationship without becoming a full makeover.
- **Physical condition:** dirt, wetness, sweat, damage and repairs have a cause, location and continuing state. Do not add random distressing to signify authenticity.
- **Return to a familiar place:** an old garment or habit may return, but avoid making every personal story depend on a symbolic accessory.

## Generate meaningful alternatives

When options are useful, provide two or three complete looks with different dramatic readings, rather than merely changing colour:

1. What the character chooses to project.
2. Exact clothing and styling choices that create that impression.
3. A personal or practical detail that complicates it.
4. How the look behaves in the required movement and photographic setting.
5. What would need to change in existing references or continuity if selected.

If the user has supplied the exact outfit, build and test that look first. Do not replace it with the stylist's preferred aesthetic. For a later revision, state affected garments, shots and continuity states.

## Examples of more useful instructions

- Instead of “dress him rich”: “He wants to appear unbothered by the formal occasion. Choose the requested understated direction: a softly structured jacket, precise trouser fit and a carefully maintained pair of shoes. Specify fabrics and colours after matching the scene palette.”
- Instead of “make her traditional”: “Identify the chosen community, place and occasion; research the relevant garments and wearing method. Develop a complete look for this individual's taste, with sourced details and clearly marked proposals.”
- Instead of “a futuristic outfit”: “Define the character's work and environment, then the garments' material and closure logic. Retain a coherent silhouette and show how the sleeves, footwear and carried equipment support the action.”
- Instead of “the same outfit later”: “Keep LOOK_A garments and accessory sides; carry forward the unfastened top button and damp right cuff caused in scene 3. Outer coat remains off and held in the left hand until its next explicit transfer.”
