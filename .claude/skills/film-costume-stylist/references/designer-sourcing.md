# Designer garments and accessories

Use this workflow when a real label helps express taste, occasion, aspiration, profession or a requested fashion direction. The brief may call for luxury, emerging designers, archive fashion or a mixture of designer and everyday pieces. Do not automatically make every character expensive or logo-heavy.

1. Establish the look's story purpose and aesthetic before searching. Preserve existing look IDs and identify any proposed revision.
2. Search official designer collections/product pages for garments and supporting shoes, bags, belts, jewellery, eyewear, watches and headwear. Use archive or museum evidence for past collections; use credible retailers when the official product page is unavailable, and label the source accordingly.
3. Inspect images as well as text. Record the exact item name, designer, collection/date if evidenced, product code if available, colourway, material, silhouette, closures and recognisable details. Never invent a product based on a label's general reputation.
4. Build a complete look with accessory scale, layering and anatomical side. Explain how it supports this person's choices. One designer item can coexist with unbranded basics; full designer styling remains valid when requested.
5. Keep a source ledger: URL, checked date, what the source verifies, image/reference role, and whether the item is selected or only an option. Record price, currency, size and region-specific availability only when useful and actually checked. A search snippet is not evidence of current stock.
6. Translate the look into visible instructions: cut, fabric, hue, hardware, stitching, proportions, logo location and wear. Bind it to a separate canonical character portrait. A generated approximation must not be described as an exact product photograph. If the exact logo or hardware matters, inspect it in the generated reference and again at final video resolution.
7. Track accessory continuity through movement: glasses on/off, watch wrist, bag shoulder, jewellery count, coat closures and contact. Complex patterns and tiny branding need checks, not an automatic ban.

Research and generation remain distinct from shopping. Do not purchase, contact a supplier or represent a designer as a sponsor without an applicable user request. Do not add a branded possession merely to explain a character's entire history.
