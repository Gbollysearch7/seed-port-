# Cuts, transitions and editorial coverage

A practical vocabulary for planning and editing films. This covers the main families rather than claiming there is one exhaustive, universally named list. Several labels can describe the same edit: a reaction cut can also be a hard picture cut with an L-cut in the audio.

Start with what the viewer should notice, understand or feel next. Choose the technique afterward. A wide shot or close-up is a framing choice; a pan, dolly or rack focus happens within a shot; a cut joins shots. Shot/reverse-shot and montage describe patterns of coverage or assembly, not transition plugins.

## Direct cuts and continuity

| Technique | What it does / useful purpose | Coverage and prompt instruction | Edit check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straight / hard cut | Instantly replaces one picture with another; can be unobtrusive or emphatic | Name outgoing and incoming views, then the event that motivates the switch | The new image adds useful information; no accidental black frame or repeated action |
| Cutting on action / match on action | Continues an action across a change of view | Describe the same action phase in both shots: same hand, object, direction and stage of contact | Match progress and momentum; avoid repeating the reach or skipping contact |
| Eyeline match | Connects a look with what the character sees | Establish the look's target, height and screen direction; supply a compatible target view | Viewpoint and direction agree; a new shot is not automatically a literal POV |
| Shot/reverse-shot | Alternates compatible views in an interaction | Establish positions and axis; obtain both speaking and listening coverage | Maintain eyelines and distances; do not cut automatically at every speaker change |
| Reaction cut | Shows the person affected by a line or event | Give the listener a specific realisation, resistance or decision; provide time for it to develop | Reaction follows its cause; incoming face does not reset to neutral |
| Cutaway | Leaves the main action for related information, then may return | Show an already established observer, location detail or relevant event | It belongs to this moment and place; the main action can plausibly continue offscreen |
| Insert / cut-in | Isolates a detail inside the action, such as a hand or object | Specify exact prop state, scale and moment; include enough context to understand it | Match ownership, hand, orientation and action phase; do not introduce unreadable text as vital evidence |
| POV sequence | Links a character's attention to their subjective view and often back to a response | Define who owns the viewpoint and where their eyes are; establish the target | Preserve believable height and angle; avoid accidentally changing to another character's POV |
| Wide / re-establishing cut | Restores geography or reveals changed relationships | Show actual positions, routes and important landmarks at this stage of the scene | The wide agrees with preceding close coverage; use when spatial information matters |
| Axial cut / discrete change in scale | Moves to a closer or wider view along approximately the same viewing axis | Request two distinct framings with coherent perspective and subject state | Make the change intentional; a slight accidental mismatch can look like a jump |

## Time, association and contrast

| Technique | What it does / useful purpose | Coverage and prompt instruction | Edit check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphic match cut | Links shots through a similar shape, composition or visual pattern | Plan the object's screen position, size and orientation in both images | Match the intended graphic relationship; this may bridge different places or times |
| Movement match | Links different actions through a shared motion or trajectory | Specify outgoing and incoming motion direction, rhythm and location in frame | Keep the visual association readable; do not imply continuous physical action unless intended |
| Jump cut | Makes a discontinuity within similar framing noticeable, often compressing time | Plan the omitted interval and changed pose or action state | The jump serves compression, agitation or style; do not call every continuity error intentional |
| Elliptical cut | Omits a period or part of an action that the audience can infer | Establish the action's beginning and a comprehensible later state | Preserve necessary cause and consequence; an ellipse need not use a jump cut |
| Cross-cutting | Alternates separate action strands to create connection, tension or convergence | Establish each strand's place, stakes and intended time relationship | Keep the relationship understandable; do not imply simultaneous events accidentally |
| Parallel editing | Juxtaposes strands for comparison or thematic connection | Specify the comparison and whether events are simultaneous or separated in time | Usage overlaps with cross-cutting; state intent instead of relying on the label alone |
| Montage | Assembles selected moments to compress a process or build an idea | Identify progression and the role of each image; vary duration by content | The sequence develops, rather than repeating decorative shots or cutting only to every beat |
| Smash cut | Makes a forceful contrast between adjacent images, situations or energy levels | Specify the expectation being interrupted and the new image that overturns it | Choose whether sound also contrasts; a loud impact is not compulsory |
| Flashback / flash-forward cut | Changes story time | Define the entry cue, time relationship, information gained and return point | Time shift is readable; sepia, blur and dissolves are optional, not required codes |
| Subjective / associative cut | Connects an image to thought, memory or perception | Identify whose experience governs the association and why this image appears now | Preserve intended ambiguity without unintentionally confusing literal scene geography |

Cross-cutting can connect separate actions and build tension; the detailed dramatic uses here are working choices for this production workflow. [Adobe: cross-cutting](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/cross-cut.html).

## Sound relative to the picture cut

- **J-cut:** incoming shot/scene audio begins while the outgoing picture remains. It can introduce a voice, location or event before revealing its source.
- **L-cut:** outgoing shot/scene audio continues after the incoming picture appears. It can let a line play over its listener's response.
- Both are split edits: picture and audio boundaries occur at different times. They are not audio crossfades by definition. [Adobe: J and L cuts](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html).

**Model-neutral example with separate clocks:**

```text
L-cut: picture changes from Daniel to Leah at 4.0 s.
Daniel's exact line continues audibly until 5.2 s.
Leah listens in the incoming shot; she does not mouth his words.
Leah begins her reply at 5.6 s, after the line has landed.

J-cut: Leah's reply starts at 8.0 s while Daniel remains visible listening.
Picture changes to Leah at 8.7 s, partway through that same reply.
Her voice and sentence continue without restarting at the picture cut.
```

These are example offsets, not fixed durations to use everywhere. Preserve each source's mouth/audio relationship when extending or trimming audio; an arbitrary slip of synchronised dialogue is not a sound bridge.

**Other sound connections:** an ambience bridge maintains a space across edits; an audio match links related sounds across images; a deliberate sound interruption can punctuate a turn. Specify source, start/end, perspective and whether speech overlaps. Match levels without flattening performance dynamics. Available clean audio handles determine what can be built.

## Concealed joins and transitions

| Technique | Intended use | Required planning and caution |
|---|---|---|
| Occlusion / hidden cut | Conceals a boundary behind an object or a temporarily covered frame | Match the covering surface, motion, direction, light and reveal; inspect the actual join in motion |
| Whip-pan transition | Connects shots through rapid panning blur | A whip pan is camera motion; the join between compatible blurred frames is the edit. Match direction, speed, blur and recovery |
| Dissolve / cross-dissolve | Blends two pictures over time; can suggest passage, association or a chosen softer connection | Needs useful overlapping material. Check double faces, hands, competing motion and changed runtime |
| Fade in / fade out | Moves between a picture and a solid colour, commonly black | Decide the ending or beginning beat and include its duration in the runtime |
| Dip to black / white | Passes through a solid frame between images | Choose for deliberate punctuation or style; it interrupts visual continuity rather than hiding a generation seam |
| Wipe / iris | Replaces or reveals the picture along a moving boundary or shape | Motivate within the visual language; it is visibly stylised unless naturally hidden by foreground action |
| Match dissolve | Combines a graphic relationship with a blend | Requires both an intentional match and useful overlap; do not mistake a morphing face for a successful match |

A direct cut and an applied transition are different operations. A dissolve uses blended imagery; available source handles affect its implementation. [Adobe: transitions overview](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-transitions/transitions-overview.html).

Speed ramps, freeze frames, split screens, motion interpolation and digital zooms can affect an edit, but are not cut types. Use them as explicit stylistic or technical choices and check sync, physical motion and delivery resolution. A one-take or held shot remains a valid alternative to any cut.

## Choosing and preparing a cut

1. State the audience's next point of attention and why it changes now.
2. Choose the outgoing performance moment and incoming information. Include both actors' physical and emotional states.
3. Establish compatible framing, eyelines, axis, perspective, light and screen direction. A substantial change of angle or scale can read more clearly than nearly matching views; no numerical angle rule guarantees a good edit.
4. Specify picture time and audio time separately. For action matches, identify the same action phase rather than just a matching final pose.
5. Obtain useful coverage and source handles. A held endpoint is useful for some continuations; a moving match needs motion through the boundary. Do not stop every action for two seconds because a previous project used that hold.
6. Keep editorial shots separate from generation jobs. One job may include multiple requested cuts; another may supply a single shot. Judge and edit the delivered footage rather than assuming requested timing was obeyed.
7. Compare the cut with holding the shot longer. More cuts do not inherently mean more cinematic work.
8. Review the join at normal speed with sound and neighbouring beats. Then inspect individual frames or audio boundaries if something feels wrong.

## For generated clips and CapCut assembly

- Save a selected-take list before assembly; do not mix incompatible identity, wardrobe or dialogue branches.
- Record source in/out ranges and sequence positions in the [edit plan](../assets/edit-plan.md). Keep untrimmed originals.
- A generated “last frame” may differ from the actual last picture used in the edit. Use the actual selected outgoing state when planning a continuation.
- Compare trimming a repeated opening hold, using a clean new angle, an existing reaction shot, or a sound bridge before adding a visible transition to hide a seam.
- In the editor, verify the actual timeline tracks, trim points, audio linkage and final playback. Do not claim a native CapCut edit from a locally prepared master alone; record where the work happened.
- Keep requested resolution and cadence. Do not enlarge a low-resolution image or add interpolation merely to make a cut appear more elaborate.
- Prompt writing and edit planning do not authorise new paid footage. If necessary coverage is absent, identify the gap and a usable fallback from existing material.
