# Live-action realism rules

For scenes intended to feel performed and photographed, use these as direction and review criteria. Preserve the project's genre and chosen visual style. They are not a guarantee that a generated take will be indistinguishable from filmed footage.

Write the few rules relevant to the shot into its prompt. Use the complete set to review the result; do not paste an exhaustive list of exclusions into every request.

## 1. Behaviour has a cause

- Give each important response a trigger and a purpose. The character hears, sees or realises something, then changes tactic. Do not anticipate information the character does not yet have.
- Direct the listener's attention and decision as well as the speaker's delivery. Avoid alternating animated speakers and frozen listeners.
- Allow character-specific hesitation, uneven thought lengths and recovery where motivated. Do not prescribe a stammer, blink, head tilt, swallow or sigh at fixed intervals to simulate humanity.
- Preserve requested emotional range. An outburst can be loud and physically expressive; naturalism does not mean everyone whispers or remains still.
- Finish or interrupt gestures for a reason. Do not loop nodding, smiling, hand motions or pacing through unrelated lines.
- Let the consequence survive the peak: changed distance, breath, gaze or an unfinished answer. The next shot inherits that pressure.

## 2. Bodies have weight and contact

- Specify a route and endpoint for important movement. Feet meet the established surface; balance and weight transfer support the step, turn, sit, rise or lean.
- A reaching hand has a target. Contact precedes lifting or moving an object; support and grip persist until release. Track which hand owns the object after the action.
- Props retain shape, size and number. A phone does not merge with fingers, a strap does not pass through a shoulder, and an object does not teleport between hands.
- Cloth, hair and accessories respond to the same body movement and environment. Do not add constant wind to manufacture activity.
- Design action for this character's body and abilities. Judge physical continuity, not conformity to one gait or posture.
- Do not hide every hand or remove all walking to avoid generation difficulty. Give important actions adequate time and readable coverage; repair failures specifically.

## 3. The same person remains on screen

- Bind portrait, wardrobe and prop references to a named character and version. Select one approved take branch before generating a continuation.
- Maintain recognisable facial structure, hair, teeth, eyes and relevant clothing details through expressions, head turns and changes of view. Distinguish normal perspective and expression changes from identity drift.
- Skin and fabric should retain coherent detail over time at the delivered resolution. Watch for waxy smoothing, crawling patterns, flickering pores, unstable teeth and features that change when partly obscured.
- Do not add exaggerated pores, random blemishes or sharpening to prove realism. Aim for the scene's appropriate texture and lighting, not maximum detail everywhere.

## 4. Speech belongs to the visible performance

- Bind exact dialogue to the correct speaker. The listener does not silently mouth the other person's words; offscreen speech retains the offscreen speaker's identity.
- Mouth movement, jaw effort, breath, pauses and expression should fit the actual audio. Inspect phrase starts, stops and visible closures in playback. A transcript is not a lip-sync check.
- Raised speech should have the chosen vocal effort and bodily response. Increasing audio gain does not repair a quietly performed outburst.
- Preserve selected voice identity, accent, pronunciation and recording perspective through cuts. Allow expressive pitch and force to vary naturally.
- Keep prompt instructions, narration and additional words out of spoken dialogue unless explicitly part of the script. Treat suspected extra speech as something to listen to and verify, not automatically as a transcription fact.
- Breaths, exertion, laughs or cries must belong to the action and character; do not decorate every line with them.

## 5. The scene occupies one coherent place

- Track screen direction, eyelines, actor distance, height relationships and positions of doors, furniture and background landmarks.
- A reverse angle reveals the same location from another position. It must not quietly move a bench, rotate a room or introduce a new layout.
- Preserve offscreen states too: the hidden actor's hands, props, posture and route still exist.
- Change the axis or geography only when intentionally staged and made understandable to the audience. A familiar continuity rule is not a ban on a purposeful visual choice.
- Carry the actual preceding frame and performance state forward when using frame continuation; a planned prompt is not evidence that the model reached that state.

## 6. Photography follows a consistent visual plan

- Specify framing, camera position, source light and focus priority. Lens numbers and camera-brand names are shorthand, not proof of a physical capture process.
- Keep light direction, shadows, reflections, skin tone and exposure compatible across adjoining shots. An angle change can change what is visible without changing the time of day.
- Make camera movement serve a subject, reveal or change of attention. Maintain a coherent path and background parallax; watch for a floating viewpoint or a background that bends around a moving actor.
- Keep focus behaviour connected to subject distance and the intended attention shift. Avoid unintended focus pumping or a background that behaves like a cut-out around hair.
- Motion blur and cadence should remain consistent with the scene's chosen movement. Normal blur is not a defect to sharpen away.
- Handheld shake, shallow focus, grain, flare and dirt are optional visual choices. Adding them cannot repair weak acting, morphing anatomy or broken geography.

## 7. Cuts preserve the ongoing event

- Cut for a response, revelation, change of tactic, meaningful action or spatial information. The end of a generation is not automatically the right place for a picture cut.
- Match emotional momentum, physical action, eyeline and prop state. A matching silhouette alone does not make a convincing continuation.
- Treat opening and closing holds as editing handles. Retain a useful pause; remove a duplicated pause that makes the character appear to restart.
- A two-second living hold means deliberate gesture and camera movement settle while breathing and attention continue. Use it where the project requests it, not at every cut in every film.
- When nearly matching frames still jump, compare clean trims, the next motivated angle or suitable existing reaction coverage. Choose by playback. Do not assume a dissolve will conceal the jump: it can create doubled faces or hands.
- Do not insert a freeze, morph, slow motion or transition merely to cover a defect without checking its effect on the scene and sync.

## 8. Sound connects the shots

- Maintain a coherent location ambience and listener perspective. Avoid an unexplained switch in wind, room tone, background voices or reverberation at a join.
- Preserve meaningful breaths and intelligible word endings. Place crossfades in suitable handles; do not overlap important dialogue accidentally.
- Match dialogue levels while retaining intended dynamic differences. A quiet admission and a shout should not become the same performance after processing.
- Footsteps, contact, cloth and prop sounds need plausible timing and material character. Do not add Foley to actions that never occur.
- Music, if part of the brief, supports the event. It does not substitute for emotion that is missing from the acting.

## 9. Prompt only decisions the shot needs

Prioritise: **dramatic action → speaker and reference bindings → essential physical continuity → photographic choices → endpoint**. Keep only applicable constraints and the most important known failure exclusions. Keep instructions separate from literal dialogue.

Avoid using “not AI,” “Oscar-level,” “ultra-real,” “8K” or a long negative list as the entire realism strategy. Describe the specific result and verify it. Requested resolution belongs in actual output settings; do not upscale or change a user's delivery choice to imply better realism.

**Compact example — unrendered, adapt before use:**

> Preserve the selected character faces, wardrobe, voices and established field. Daniel keeps the phone in his right hand at his thigh. He hears Leah finish her accusation before answering; his raised defence grows from that trigger, with one left-hand gesture that settles after the line. Leah listens, then responds to the meaning of his words rather than copying his movements. Maintain grounded weight shifts, consistent eyelines and the same bench position through the planned camera angles. Bind each exact line to its speaker; keep the listener silent during offscreen dialogue. Preserve the daylight direction and continuous field ambience. End with the unresolved reaction still present; breathing continues during the requested hold.

This supplements the shot's actual script and timing. It is not a substitute for them or a claim that the resulting take has passed review.

## 10. Evaluate the take and the assembled scene

Use the [take and sequence review](../assets/take-review.md). Watch with sound at normal speed for believability first; inspect suspect moments more closely afterward. Also watch joins in context. A contact sheet cannot establish smooth movement, acting rhythm, voice identity or sound continuity.

| Observed defect | First correction to consider |
|---|---|
| Character seems unresponsive | Clarify what lands and what decision follows; show that response in suitable coverage |
| Busy but unconvincing movement | Keep the gesture with a purpose; remove unrelated loops and give the action an endpoint |
| Foot slide or impossible object contact | Correct weight, route, grip and contact timing; allow sufficient time and useful framing |
| Face, hands or clothing change through motion | Check reference conflicts and the affected action; revise that take or use verified alternative coverage |
| Wrong mouth moves or extra words appear | Tighten speaker/line binding; verify actual speech before deciding a repair |
| Repeated voice or accent change | Reuse the selected identity/session anchor and compare adjacent lines |
| Join exposes a reset | Match actual outgoing state, remove duplicate lead-in or cut to a motivated alternate angle |
| Flickering light, focus or texture | Simplify conflicting photographic cues and compare against the established look |
| Audio changes room at a cut | Match perspective and ambience using available compatible material |

Separate observed defects from suspicions. Preserve successful takes and make the smallest useful correction. Record unresolved issues and the scope of review; do not label an unviewed result “artifact-free.” A review finding is not automatic authorisation for paid rerenders.
