# Voice and pacing prompt patterns

Use the [voice, accent and pacing sheet](../assets/voice-accent-pacing.md) to select an identity before choosing delivery. These are unrendered examples, not instructions to recast existing characters or generate audio.

## Build an original voice

Choose a few compatible audible traits and a character-specific rhythm. For example:

> VOICE ID: CHR_A_V01. [Chosen language and approved accent baseline]. Mid-low register, warm resonance with lightly textured endings, clear word endings. At ease, thoughts arrive in compact phrases. Preserve the recognisable timbre and accent as force and expressive pitch change.

Another character might use brighter resonance, a wider expressive range and longer flowing phrases. Choose for the individual, not automatically by gender, nationality or status. Avoid contradictory adjective piles such as “deep, thin, booming, delicate, breathless and perfectly smooth.”

## Specify accent without caricature

Replace an underspecified direction such as “a strong British accent” with a completed casting record:

> Language: English. Accent baseline: [chosen regional variety]. Prominence: [relative to approved anchor]. Anchor: [take and time range, or explicitly untested text design]. Recurring words: [approved pronunciations]. Maintain the baseline through emotional changes. Register or language switches: [only changes motivated by the scene].

If the region is unspecified, keep it as a casting choice instead of inventing one. An accent label does not authorise slang or rewritten dialogue. Verify precise pronunciation features when needed rather than relying on a stereotype. Clear speech and a pronounced accent can coexist.

## Delivery choices

| Scene need | Useful direction | Listening / recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled authority | Finish the thought deliberately; land the decision word without forcing a deeper voice | Let the partner absorb it; avoid a pause after every word |
| Defensive anger | Start as if dismissing the point; pressure and force grow when challenged | Retain effort after the line instead of instantly resetting |
| Hurt beneath composure | Attempt ordinary delivery; a specific word interrupts the flow; continue with a changed tactic | The partner's response determines whether composure holds |
| Urgency / fear | Group words around the task; accelerate toward the warning and articulate the crucial instruction | Check whether the other person acts |
| Excitement | Thoughts gather speed as the news becomes real; pitch can expand without changing identity | A response may interrupt the excitement |
| Tenderness | Address this person at the established distance; soften attack and make reassurance specific | Do not automatically whisper or exaggerate breathiness |
| Dry humour | Commit to the setup; let recognition land after the operative word | Follow meaning rather than a fixed comedy pause |
| Disbelief | Stop the prepared reply; the next words test what was heard | Allow verification before the next tactic |

## Pacing an exchange

**Unrendered 12-second rehearsal example.** Retain selected voices, accents and blocking. This changes delivery only.

| Time | Action and speech | Delivery and edit purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 s | Daniel receives the preceding accusation | A quick intake prepares a defence; inherit the prior emotion |
| 1–4 s | DANIEL: “I never asked you to do any of that!” | Brisk, raised defence; stress “asked.” Keep the whole line intelligible and let the final word complete |
| 4–5 s | Leah listens and chooses her reply | A processing beat, not an empty pause |
| 5–8 s | LEAH: “You didn't have to ask. You ate, didn't you?” | Meet his force; briefly let the first sentence land, then stress “ate” as a fact he cannot evade |
| 8–10 s | Daniel cannot sustain the defence | A listener shot can show the unspoken attempted answer and residual breath |
| 10–12 s | Living hold | No new line; breathing and attention continue |

These are rehearsal windows. If actual speech does not fit, revise timing rather than assuming the actor or model will compress it naturally. The hold belongs to this example, not every exchange.

## Rhythm through picture edits

- **Speech over a reaction:** keep the speaker audible while showing the listener. Bind the offscreen line to the correct speaker; the visible listener stays silent.
- **Incoming speech before a picture cut:** begin the next speaker while still on the previous listener only when intentional and understandable.
- **Ambience through a cut:** carry the location sound across the join, matching perspective without masking consonants or breaths.
- **Continuation:** carry the same thought, accent, effort and distance forward. Avoid duplicated words, unexplained room changes or emotional resets.

Mixing can match levels and smooth a sound boundary. It cannot turn calm reading into an embodied argument or guarantee speaker consistency.
