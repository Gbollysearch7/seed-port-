## Scope and limits

The user requested films above 100,000 views, prioritising millions. The fixed set was the 17 qualifying films exposed in the sidebar of The Janitor They Humiliated Was Secretly the CEO. Each was opened in its own Chrome tab and kept open. Recommendations on those new pages were not recursively added. Two previously studied films supply additional evidence, giving 19 references overall.

Six available transcripts were read from beginning to end: the two earlier films and the four highest-view additions. The other thirteen were inspected through opening, middle and ending passages plus selected causal turns. This is transcript research with limited visual inspection, not complete viewing of nineteen films. Automatic captions sometimes omit action, merge speakers or vary names. Interpretations needing unobserved action remain uncertain.

Counts below were observed on landing pages during this session; they are snapshots, not forecasts. Ranking used the rounded sidebar counts, which explains later exact-count differences. The personal recommendation set is biased toward successful examples and contains no matched failures. No retention, click-through rate, traffic-source, revenue or promotion data was available. Therefore “working” here means an observed craft device with a plausible appeal, not a demonstrated cause of views.

Made-with-AI disclosures establish only that sounds or visuals were altered/generated. No verified model credit established the user's proposed Google model attribution. STALLED has live-action filmmaker/actor credits and a 2022 upload; it belongs in a separate comparison category.

## Detailed transcript findings

### STALLED — approximately 19:55

A selfish executive hurries to a meeting, dismisses a janitor and makes threatening statements. A restroom time paradox forces him to confront versions of himself who apply the same selfish logic. At about 6:45, the duplicate explains the trap; around 9–13 minutes, incompatible strategies and self-interest multiply danger; around 14:20–17 minutes, the paradox reaches its lethal confrontation. The late transcript contains mostly music, so exact nonverbal escape mechanics cannot be reconstructed from captions alone. The creator synopsis describes hard-won change. Comments propose additional interpretations; these are not verified plot facts.

Transfer: make a flaw generate obstacles rather than merely label the character. The setting constrains the puzzle, and repeated situations change through consequences. Do not equate a three-year-old short on a large established channel with a recent AI channel's performance.

### School helicopter film — 47:13

The opening establishes class humiliation, damage to a sentimental necklace and romantic betrayal. The principal recognises Mia before the bullies do; helicopter/parentage recognition arrives around 7–9 minutes. At 10:00, the film moves back to explain her experiment with appearing poor. Her defence of a cleaner demonstrates values; a photograph of her with her father is misrepresented; her boyfriend's private behaviour confirms manipulation. A retail confrontation implicates the bully's mother and leads to documented complaints.

The final stretch revisits the revelation, rejects the boyfriend's opportunistic regret, and distinguishes consequences for misconduct from punishment for disrespecting wealth. Mia keeps agency by declining reunion and asking for accountability based on actions. Necklace, bike and expensive shoes recur with changed meaning. The opening deliberately gives away the status reversal: the middle explains how trust was lost, and the end judges the apologies. Wealth remains a convenient source of protection; the transferable moral test must also work for an actually poor student.

### Prince and private chef — 40:47

Francis is pressured to marry within 90 days, while Eva's father rejects her chosen vocation. These parallel family pressures support the romance. A breakup and an unexpected maternal visit trigger the pretend relationship around 9–12 minutes. The mother's extended stay turns a brief cover story into shared time; genuine attraction becomes explicit around 21–23 minutes. The ex's return and hidden royal identity create new obstacles. Around 29 minutes, Eva refuses the proposal. The ending reconciles parental control, secrecy and mutual commitment, and celebrates her work as well as marriage.

Transfer: a practical lie creates opportunities for intimacy, then threatens the thing it enabled. Both leads need goals outside romance. Weaknesses to resist: repetitive declarations, the ex reduced largely to spending/entitlement, and an accidental missed flight supplying the reconciliation opportunity. A new story should make honest action earn reunion and reciprocal compatibility earn love.

### Waitress and chairman — 39:45

Nkiru defends an older customer's right to remain and loses work before learning his status. At about 3–4 minutes, the family investigates why a manager obeyed an entitled customer; the waitress's husband turns out to be involved in an earlier fraud accusation. The story expands into altered payment authorisation, an executive terminal, a complicit family and competing claims about protection. Around 18–20 minutes, confidentiality/settlement pressure makes truth personally costly and a first accountability sequence clears theft while retaining a procedural mistake.

That apparent resolution opens a second strand: concealed former engagement, paternity evidence and falsified information. By 35–38 minutes, blackmail links the strands, and the seemingly helpful executive admits selectively releasing information through the wife. The available transcript ends around 39:38 with another problem instead of resolving the newest one.

Transfer: connect public mistreatment to a larger system; let allies also be accountable; make new evidence reinterpret prior actions. Risk: the advertised kindness-reward premise becomes a much larger secret network and loses standalone closure. Do not claim the full story is resolved based on this transcript.

### Poor maiden versus arranged marriage — approximately 45:09

The opening previews destruction of Amanda's livelihood and a bribe, then returns three weeks earlier. Frank sees her feed someone hungry before romance develops. Family business interests support an arranged match and escalating pressure. Her lost shop/home/phone interrupt contact. Around 29–30 minutes, Frank risks inheritance and loses access to family assets. A friend locates Amanda; reunion and parental acceptance close the story around 39–45 minutes.

Useful: readable love-versus-status conflict and kindness demonstrated through action. Weaknesses: repeated marriage arguments and subscribe interruptions, shifting deadlines, unexplained asset continuity, an unresolved visitor/fraud strand, and a late contract solution that reduces the father's cost of accepting the couple. Retain the emotional engine and repair those mechanics.

### Undercover janitor/CEO — approximately 49:39

Humiliation opens the film. At 1:11, business/customer/staff indicators justify investigation; the audience knows the chairwoman's cover by 2:22. Lena demonstrates skill through customer advice and kindness through sharing lunch. Escalation changes form: reduced hours, unpaid work, withheld bonuses, manipulated time records and retaliatory performance reviews. A frightened assistant manager becomes an evidence source. Around 34–36 minutes, Lena is fired and speaks for herself; authority is recognised around 37:52–38:29.

The payoff includes documented removal, restored compensation, complaint review and an earned development assessment. The last minutes show changed practice and a callback to Lena's childhood relationship with fashion. Transfer: combine dramatic irony, evidence, protagonist agency, practical repair and aftermath. Caveat: long intervention delays require credible justification; a stated protective reason does not prove every minute is necessary.

## Synthesis for the skill

Observed recurring mechanisms: immediate unfairness; recognisable economic or family pressure; an action proving character; unequal control; concealed information; changing retaliation; a costly choice; public correction; concrete restoration; and a callback showing changed life. Not every film uses every mechanism.

Plausible audience appeal: clarity about whom to root for, recognition of everyday mistreatment, anticipation of a power reversal, romance across constraints and the pleasure of seeing specific wrongs corrected. These are editorial hypotheses. A few earlier visible comments referenced controlling parents, workplace mistreatment and relief at justice; that small visible sample is not representative audience research.

The reusable skill should support several engines and improve causal coherence, choice, credibility and closure. It should not certify a particular model, duplicate plots, promote wealth as worth, or call these devices a guarantee of millions of views.
